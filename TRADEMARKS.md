# Situate trademark policy

This policy explains use of the Situate name, wordmarks, app icon, and Bend and Mini Bend artwork when they identify the source of a product or service. Its purpose is to distinguish official Situate offerings from independent ones without restricting ordinary development, discussion or artistic reuse.

Software and artwork permissions are described in [Licensing and reuse](LICENSING.md). Neither the MIT License nor CC BY 4.0 provides a general trademark license. This policy does not assert that any particular mark is registered, create trademark rights or override uses permitted by applicable law.

## Uses that do not need separate permission

Provided the presentation is truthful and does not imply an official connection, you may:

- Refer to Situate in articles, reviews, tutorials, comparisons and discussions; show screenshots or logos to identify the project.
- Say that your software or service “is based on Situate” or “is compatible with Situate,” when accurate. Use your own identity as the primary branding.
- Describe independent commercial hosting or support for Situate, with your operator identity and independent status clearly visible.
- Fork the repository, retain its name and assets for contribution and development, and build or modify local copies. A contribution fork does not need rebranding merely to propose changes.
- Self-host Situate, including with its existing interface and artwork, while making clear who operates the instance and not claiming it is an official Situate-operated service.
- Redistribute unchanged official releases under their existing name and artwork, while identifying yourself as the distributor and not claiming official status. Preserve applicable license notices.
- Reuse or modify the artwork outside Situate, including commercially, under CC BY 4.0, when it is not used as misleading product or service branding.

These permissions do not require registration, payment or individual approval. Retain the attribution and notices required by the applicable copyright licenses.

## Independent products and services

Use your own product name and primary logo when publicly offering an independently branded derivative product or competing service. You may retain Situate's interface layout, components, colors and general illustrations. You may also explain the product's origin in Situate.

If your offering could reasonably be mistaken for an official one, state clearly that it is independently operated and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Situate project. A disclaimer does not by itself cure an otherwise misleading product name, logo, domain or overall presentation.

This does not require removal of every occurrence of “Situate” from source code or documentation. Preserve copyright notices, license notices and accurate historical or technical references.

## Uses not authorized by this policy

Without separate permission, do not:

- Claim that an independent product or service is “official Situate” or is published, approved, sponsored or operated by the Situate project.
- Use the Situate name, wordmark, app icon, Bend or a confusingly similar mark as your product's primary identity in a way that suggests an official connection.
- Choose a domain, account name or overall presentation that misleadingly impersonates the project or its official services.
- Turn a required attribution into a claim of endorsement.

Bend's presence in an illustration does not by itself imply endorsement. This policy does not prohibit artistic modifications, criticism, parody or other lawful uses, and it does not reserve all visual features of the interface.

## Requests and reports

For a proposed trademark use outside these permissions, or to report a misleading claim of affiliation, use the contact details in the [Situate legal notice](https://getsituate.com/legal-notice.html). Refer to the relevant mark and describe or link to the proposed use. Do not post private correspondence or sensitive personal information in public issues.

This policy applies only to Situate's identity. Third-party names and logos remain subject to their owners' rights.
