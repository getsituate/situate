# 4. Brand voice and writing examples

Situate speaks to one person about that person's material. It is calm, attentive, direct and occasionally dryly funny. Warmth comes from paying attention to the person's situation and communicating with care.

## Sound like a helpful counterpart

- **Attentive:** respond to the actual situation.
- **Uncomplicated:** use natural language and concrete verbs.
- **Calm:** give people room to decide, without manufactured urgency.
- **Honest:** explain uncertainty plainly.
- **Lightly humorous:** make an occasional observation about the situation, keeping the person's dignity intact.

Situate respects the person's choices, pace and working habits. Write about overdue tasks, clutter and time away without judgment or pressure.

## Make the first reading count

Name the note, document, task or reply involved and what the person can do with it. A plugin can be introduced as a reusable extension adding a tool, connection or view.

Use everyday English that an international audience can follow comfortably. Explain unfamiliar terms and keep important conditions explicit. Use sentence case and direct actions: “Review draft,” “Open note,” “See an example.”

## Keep a recognizable voice across languages

Address readers as “you” in English and use informal second-person address in German. Adapt phrasing so that the intended meaning and tone feel natural in each language. Letters and emails written on someone's behalf should suit their recipient and context.

## Writing examples

**Scattered information:** “Writing the reply is quick. Finding everything you need takes longer.”

**Short product explanation:** “Situate is being built to bring your notes, tasks and documents together, and help prepare the next step from that context.”

For available prototype capabilities, use the product introduction's prototype section.

**Invitation to talk:** “Which part of keeping your notes and tasks connected takes more effort than it should?”

# Put the voice to work

## Responding to a skeptical comment

**Comment:** “AI usually gives me more work to check.”

**Possible reply:** “That review effort matters. The preparation has to save enough work to make checking it worthwhile.”

A follow-up about what needs correcting can help when the conversation calls for it.

**Dry humor:** after simplifying overlapping categories, “Miscellaneous had quite a few responsibilities” can fit. Skip jokes around sensitive matters, errors, costs, permissions and consequential decisions.

## Illustrative post

> A venue asks for the final details. The guest list is in a note, the timings are in a PDF, and the agreement is in an earlier email. The hard part is bringing it together again. That is the kind of coordination Situate is being built to help with. Where do the details of your plans usually end up?

## Precision in product wording

Name the action precisely: drafted, reviewed, sent or completed. Identify the context used and what a requested tool does. Explain app pricing and separate AI-provider costs using confirmed current terms; the audience guide summarizes the intended model. Label illustrations and name the product version in demonstrations.

Security, privacy, integrations and reliability claims need a verified scope. Describe local storage, synchronization and AI processing separately. Do not promise unlimited AI, universal integrations or error-free work. Original software and documentation use MIT; original artwork uses CC BY 4.0. Attribution and trademark rules still apply. Open-source licensing does not establish an independent security audit.

## Before publishing

- A clear situation and concrete benefit.
- Accurate product status and claims.
- Respectful language.
- A next step that matches its destination, including an available offering for any download or purchase invitation.
- Labels or qualifications needed for the material being shared, kept beside the relevant claim or image.
