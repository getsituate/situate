# 3. Audience, problems and positioning

## Who we want to reach

Our initial focus is on people who actively shape their personal organization and want their tools to reflect how they work. They choose the arrangement of their notes, tasks and commitments; Situate aims to take on the repeated filing, connecting and updating involved.

The audience spans personal and professional life, including household planning, private interests and paid work. Knowledge workers and independent professionals are among the people who may find Situate useful. Technical expertise is optional; people can describe what they need in everyday language.

## Illustrative problem statements

- “I already have the information, but I have to find it again before I can act.”
- “My notes and task list tell different versions of the same story.”
- “Every new email makes me reconstruct the whole process.”
- “The tool expects me to change a working method I like.”

The underlying themes are repeated coordination, scattered context, mental effort and poor fit between tools and habits.

## Personal organization across different methods

Situate serves one person and supports the way they choose to organize. Someone might keep tasks in project notes, use separate lists or combine established methods. The initial audience includes people with different professions, interests and routines.

Shared work stays in connected external services. Situate's own tasks, knowledge and documents form an individual organizational space.

---

# Outcomes worth talking about

- **Functional:** Less repeated searching, copying, explaining and updating; useful work prepared from existing context.
- **Automation:** Recurring email and calendar work carried out according to personal rules, without initiating every step by hand.
- **Personal:** An organizational system that reflects the person's working methods and interests.
- **Control:** The person defines the work, access and rules, can inspect results and retains consequential decisions.

These are intended benefits. Research needs to establish where they matter most and how consistently Situate can deliver them.

## Automation in everyday life

People can describe recurring work they want Situate to handle. Possible workflows include:

- **An organized personal inbox:** follow an empty-inbox approach, often called Inbox Zero, by sorting incoming messages, flagging replies needed and archiving handled messages according to the person's rules.
- **Time around meetings:** add preparation and follow-up blocks when meetings are added or moved. The person defines block lengths and how the workflow should handle overlaps or exceptions.
- **Replies prepared automatically:** use relevant notes and earlier correspondence to suggest replies to selected incoming emails or save drafts in the connected account. Sending remains a separate requested action.

The aim is to maintain the chosen arrangement as new information arrives, reducing the routine effort of keeping it useful.

## What these workflows need

These examples describe product direction. Each requires working tools, service access and agreed rules. Configured automation runs while the relevant workspace is open and the platform permits execution; closing the workspace, quitting the app or operating-system suspension can stop it. Verify a specific workflow before describing it as available.

# Turn the position into a message

> Your personal organization, connected.

> Keep tasks, knowledge and documents together. Let AI help with the routine work.

The proposed distinction combines personal context, organizational work and tools developed from individual requests. We need to test how much this combination matters to people and how it compares with the alternatives they already use.

## Lead with the benefit

Describe work Situate could take on: gathering material, updating tasks or preparing a reply. Show how a requested tool could fit the person's method—for example, a view of open tasks kept in project notes. Identify the sources, access and working code it requires.

## Cost and choice

The intended model is a meaningfully usable free local application on one device with user-provided AI access. AI providers may charge separately. Optional paid synchronization and managed AI access are planned. Prices, packages, allowances and service commitments remain open; confirm current terms before publishing an offer.

## Situations to explore

These situations provide starting points for audience research.

- A person with tasks scattered through project notes: could a combined view reduce duplicate maintenance?
- A person managing recurring administrative correspondence: where would retained context reduce repeated explanation?
- A person organizing a hobby or personal event: how do they keep documents, decisions and next steps connected?

## What we still need to learn

Research priorities include the frequency and impact of these problems, review effort, willingness to change routines or pay, differentiation from alternatives, and acquisition costs.

Ask about a recent occurrence, the tools involved, the workaround and what would make a prepared result worth reviewing. Likes and individual stories can guide further questions; demand, sustained use and willingness to pay need evidence from behavior over time.
