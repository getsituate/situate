# UX discovery

## Frame the investigation

Read the relevant goals, requirements and foundations. Choose one concrete task and the uncertainty that most affects its design. Separate reported behavior, observed behavior, expert judgment and untested assumptions.

Describe the person's trigger, available context, current workaround and desired result. Identify the start and end of the journey, decisions, handoffs and recovery points. Do not assume a screen, chat or organizational method is the solution.

## Explore structure and interaction

Identify the objects users need to recognize, their relationships, useful names and likely entry points. Compare grouping, navigation and retrieval approaches against the task, not a generic application template.

For Situate, investigate only the relevant boundaries: tasks, knowledge and documents; chat and direct manipulation; local and external content; active and other open workspaces; AI proposals and approved effects; plugin capabilities and limitations. Consult the current catalog rather than inventing permission prompts or recovery protocols.

Use `generate-variations` and `wireframe` for materially different approaches. Include entry, completion and the most consequential interruption. Compare discoverability, effort, learnability, user control, accessibility and technical uncertainty. An unfamiliar pattern needs a reason; novelty alone is not a benefit.

## Plan a small usability test

Specify:

- The assumption and what observation would support or contradict it.
- Participants relevant to the scenario and what remains unknown about their representativeness.
- A neutral task describing a goal without naming the control or desired path.
- Starting state, synthetic content and the minimum prototype fidelity needed.
- Observable outcomes: task completion, hesitation, wrong turns, recovery and understanding of consequences.
- Follow-up questions and the decision the findings could change.

Do not invent sample-size rules, pass rates or research results. Propose thresholds only as provisional choices. Separate a heuristic walkthrough from testing with participants. Recruiting, contacting or recording people requires authorization; use agreed consent and data handling when research is requested.

## Interpret and conclude

Report observations separately from explanations and recommendations. State the limits of small samples and missing evidence. Never substitute simulated personas or model-generated responses for user research.

A discovery round returns the scenario, distinct options, a reasoned recommendation, the key remaining assumption and a test for it. If the evidence does not distinguish options, say so. User preference selects a direction; it does not validate usability.

Keep raw research outside the public repository. Persist only authorized, reviewed, non-identifying summaries and synthetic artifacts. Reference existing requirement IDs without copying the catalog. Unresolved product changes remain proposals, not approved requirements.
