# Clear writing

Establish what the reader needs to understand or do. Put that information first and remove material that does not help. Use familiar terms, explicit actors, and concrete consequences rather than promotional claims or abstract benefits.

Preserve identifiers, links, technical qualifications, and the difference between observations, proposals, decisions, and verified results. Do not make a sentence more confident than its evidence permits.

Prefer short coherent paragraphs. Use lists or tables when they make comparison or lookup easier, not to decorate a simple explanation. Explain unfamiliar terms once and use them consistently.

Before finishing, remove repeated conclusions, vague adjectives, process narration, and statements about satisfying the task. Check that the result makes sense to someone who has not seen the conversation. Do not expand the document's scope merely to make it look complete.
