## Description

Situate must speak as a friendly, attentive counterpart with its own recognizable voice. The tone must be calm, direct, adult, and honest, without staged affection, routine praise, or judgment about productivity. The editorial guide is design/brand/docs/voice.md.

Routine interface text must use clear everyday language, with roughly CEFR B1 as editorial orientation rather than a mandatory score. Chat may use specialist language and greater depth when requested. German and English must preserve the same personality and meaning through natural phrasing, not literal sentence-by-sentence translation. [Dry humor](REQ-0051-use-occasional-dry-humor.md) is a separate should-have quality.

## Rationale

A recognizable voice supports personal connection while low reading effort helps users understand their work and decisions.

## Acceptance criteria

- Routine controls and messages use familiar words and concrete actions; necessary technical terms are explained without assuming technical or organizational-method expertise.
- Review of action and approval messages checks that consequences, conditions, costs, and uncertainty remain explicit rather than being removed for brevity.
- Conversation addresses the actual request without habitual praise, pet names, claims of emotional attachment, or judgments about inactivity and productivity.
- First-person statements identify actual actions or uncertainty without inventing emotions, knowledge, or completed work.
- Persistent controls remain concise and informative rather than being turned into conversational flourishes.
- Situate addresses the user with informal German second-person forms, without a separate form-of-address setting.
- Generated letters, emails, and other authored material use the recipient-appropriate tone and form of address according to context and the user's request, rather than automatically inheriting Situate's own conversational voice.
- German and English wording preserve action meaning and limits while sounding natural in each language.
- A request for specialist detail can receive a substantive answer without an artificial B1 ceiling; a readability score alone is not used as evidence of comprehension.
