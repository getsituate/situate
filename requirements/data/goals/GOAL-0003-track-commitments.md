## User problem

People who cannot rely on capturing and tracking their work must also remember commitments or repeatedly check their records. Further attention may be needed even after taking action, for example when a response does not arrive.

The individual workflow described during elicitation used GTD to build trust: collecting incoming items in inboxes, clarifying them regularly and checking by the weekly review that projects have a next action. A task overview distinguished scheduled work, actual deadlines and earliest start dates. Follow-up reminders after email replies were created according to a user-provided rule. This is an individual example, not a prescribed workflow for all users.

## Desired outcome

Users can stop keeping topics and commitments in mind and trust that these are tracked within their chosen working method. They can see what needs attention today and what to do next without reviewing every project again. Open processes whose next step depends on someone else remain visible for follow-up.

## Success criteria

- Captured open topics reappear during the intended review rather than merely being stored and forgotten.
- Users can identify today's relevant work and next actions in their task overview without opening every project's documents.
- When the user's workflow includes scheduled work, actual due dates and earliest start dates, their distinct meanings remain intact in the overview. These are not mandatory default fields; [task workflow adaptation](../requirements/REQ-0034-adapt-task-workflows.md) and [import conversion](../requirements/REQ-0036-convert-imported-notes-and-tasks.md) preserve personal field semantics.
- Where follow-up has been agreed, a pending reply becomes visible at the intended review time without the user having to remember it separately.
- Users feel less need to track commitments in memory or on a parallel checklist. This benefit needs validation beyond the individual account.

## Open questions

- What reliable review and follow-up practices do target users employ outside GTD?
- Which date and follow-up rules do users specify, and which may be inferred from their context?
- Beyond subjective trust, how can we verify that open commitments actually become visible in time?
