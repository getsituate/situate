## User problem

Long-running organizational processes require collecting documents, checking requirements and communicating with multiple parties. New follow-up questions force users to reconstruct the current state and personal circumstances if context is unavailable.

An individual prototype experience involved a multi-party administrative process with documents and correspondence. New emails could be handled together with the prototype without explaining previously documented context again. The user's contribution mainly involved reviewing and sending drafts, making small wording changes and adding information not yet available digitally. This is a reported individual experience, not a measured general capability.

## Desired outcome

Users handle complex organizational processes with less personal processing time and mental effort. Situate uses existing personal context, documents and prior history to carry out the necessary work up to a reviewable result. New requests can build on the existing state. Users add missing information, review results and make the necessary decisions.

[Decision authority](GOAL-0004-decision-authority.md) remains with the user.

## Success criteria

- A new follow-up question about an ongoing process can be handled without the user explaining already available context again.
- A prepared result enables final review and decision-making rather than requiring the user to repeat research and compilation.
- Previously unavailable information, such as information from a conversation, can be added and used in subsequent work.
- Users report less personal processing time and mental effort than in their previous workflow. Quantitative baselines and targets are not yet available.

## Source

Private goal elicitation conversation; date not recorded, transcript not included. Topics: the most valued prototype benefit; jointly handling new correspondence; reviewing drafts, adjusting wording and adding conversational context; reported reductions in time and mental effort. Personal circumstances have been omitted; this remains a single self-reported experience.
