## User problem

Functional usefulness alone does not establish whether users feel personally connected to their organizational system. A system that does not reflect their working method or personality, or interactions in which they do not feel understood, may feel impersonal rather than their own.

This is a concern underlying the requested experience, not evidence of dissatisfaction with the current application or a measured problem across users.

## Desired outcome

Users experience Situate and their personal organizational system as something of their own with which they feel emotionally connected. The system reflects their working method and personality; in their interactions with Situate, they feel understood and supported.

## Success criteria

- Users can describe specific aspects of their system that reflect their working method and personality and make it feel personally fitting.
- Users can identify situations in which interacting with Situate made them feel understood or supported.
- Users describe a sense of personal connection to Situate and their organizational system. Evaluation methods, baselines and target levels remain to be established.

## Source

Private goal elicitation conversation; date not recorded, transcript not included. Emotional identification with the application and one's own organizational system was requested. Both reflection of working method and personality and a sense of being understood and supported were confirmed. The goal and qualitative success criteria were explicitly approved for inclusion.

## Open questions

- Which aspects of working method and personality matter most to users' sense of connection?
- Which interactions foster a feeling of being understood and supported, and which feel intrusive or inappropriate?
- How should this experience be evaluated across users and over time?
